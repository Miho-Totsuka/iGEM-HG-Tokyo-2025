# iGEM HG-Tokyo 2025

## Overview

In 2025, I participated in iGEM as a member of HG-Tokyo, a high school team competing in the iGEM High School Section.

Our project focused on developing new approaches to hay fever using narirutin biosynthesis and a fusion peptide.

### Team

* Team: HG-Tokyo
* Competition: iGEM 2025 — High School Section

### Team Achievements

* Gold Medal
* Best Presentation — High School, Nominee
* Best Integrated Human Practices — High School, Nominee
* Best Village Project — High School, Nominee

## Project Overview

Our project addressed hay fever through two complementary approaches:

1. Narirutin Biosynthesis
2. Fusion Peptide

For the narirutin approach, we aimed to biosynthesize narirutin, a flavonoid mainly found in Jabara, using yeast.

For the fusion peptide approach, we designed a fusion peptide using immunomodulatory peptides and cleavable linkers.

Our overall goal was to develop a comprehensive approach to hay fever and ultimately explore how these approaches could be incorporated into a nasal spray.

# My Roles & Contributions

I contributed to the project in four main areas:

* Education
* International Collaboration
* Project Ideation
* Finance

## 1. Education

### Role: Education Contributor

I contributed to educational outreach activities aimed at making synthetic biology more accessible to students and the general public.

I gave introductory lectures to elementary and junior high school students, adapting the content and explanations for younger audiences with limited scientific background.

I also created YouTube videos addressing common questions and concerns about synthetic biology, particularly its potential risks and safety. My goal was to explain these topics in a way that people without a scientific background could understand.

Through these activities, I gained experience communicating the same scientific topic in different ways depending on the age, background, and level of scientific knowledge of the audience.

### Lab Workshop

I helped organize and support a hands-on synthetic biology laboratory workshop for middle and high school students.

The workshop introduced participants to synthetic biology and basic laboratory equipment through lectures, experiments, and Q&A sessions.

By taking part in the workshop, I learned that explaining science in person is different from simply presenting scientific information. It is important to observe how participants respond, adjust explanations when necessary, and create opportunities for them to ask questions.

## 3. Project Ideation

### Utilizing Narirutin from Jabara

During the early stages of developing our project, we explored different possible approaches to addressing hay fever.

I became interested in Jabara, a citrus fruit known for containing narirutin, and began thinking about whether narirutin could be incorporated into our project.

I proposed exploring the use of narirutin as one possible approach to hay fever and contributed to discussions about how this idea could be developed further.

The team later explored the possibility of biosynthesizing narirutin using yeast as part of the project.

Through this process, I learned that an initial research idea does not immediately become a finished project. Ideas develop through discussion, investigation, feedback, and collaboration with other team members and researchers.

# Researcher Visits in Italy

## April 2025 — Naples, Italy

As part of my interest in synthetic biology and biological research, I independently contacted researchers in Naples, Italy, and arranged two laboratory visits and research interviews.

Both visits were conducted in English.

During the meetings, I learned about the researchers' work, asked questions about laboratory research, and sought advice about our own iGEM project.

Some of my teammates joined the discussions online so that we could ask more detailed questions related to our project.

Organizing these visits myself gave me experience taking the initiative to contact researchers, preparing questions, communicating professionally in English, and discussing scientific ideas with researchers working in the field.

## Telethon Institute of Genetics and Medicine (TIGEM)

* Institution: Telethon Institute of Genetics and Medicine
* Location: Naples, Italy
* Researcher: Professor Diego di Bernardo
* Activity: Laboratory visit and research interview
* Language: English

During the meeting, I asked Professor di Bernardo around ten questions about conducting laboratory experiments related to synthetic biology and about ways our team could improve its project idea.

I also asked about his research, his work as a scientist, and some of the technical challenges involved in his field.

Three or four of my teammates who had more experience with the laboratory side of our project joined the discussion online. This allowed us to ask more detailed technical questions and receive feedback related to our project.

Visiting the institute in person was especially valuable because I was able to see laboratories in operation and gain a better understanding of how research is conducted in a professional laboratory environment.

## Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT)

* Institution: Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia
* Location: Naples, Italy
* Researcher: Professor Velia Siciliano
* Activity: Laboratory visit and research interview
* Language: English

During this meeting, I interviewed Professor Siciliano about her work and the research she was conducting.

I also introduced aspects of our project and asked for her advice on our topic and ways we could improve our ideas.

As with the TIGEM meeting, several of my teammates joined the discussion online so that we could ask more detailed questions about our project.

These meetings helped me understand how researchers evaluate scientific ideas and how receiving feedback from people with different areas of expertise can help improve a project.

I learned that successful international collaboration requires preparation, communication, coordination, and a willingness to understand different perspectives.

## Research

I learned that research ideas evolve through discussion, investigation, feedback, and interaction with other people.

My experience proposing the use of narirutin and discussing our project with researchers showed me that an initial idea can change significantly as more information and perspectives are introduced.

## Project Management

I learned that a scientific project requires more than experiments.

Education, finance, communication, fundraising, scheduling, and coordination are all important parts of carrying out a large team project.

## Science and Society

This made me more aware of the importance of communicating scientific research clearly and listening to the questions and concerns of different communities.

## Looking Forward

My experience with HG-Tokyo in 2025 gave me a foundation in science communication, educational outreach, international collaboration, project organization, and scientific discussion.

In particular, working directly with students and the public helped me understand how educational activities are planned, communicated, and adapted for different audiences.

These experiences gave me a foundation that I could build on in future iGEM activities.
